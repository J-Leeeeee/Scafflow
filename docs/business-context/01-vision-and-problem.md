# Vision and Problem

## The Problem

Generative AI can finish STEM homework in seconds. For learning, that is exactly the risk: when the answer is one prompt away, students can bypass the reasoning process that homework is designed to develop.

Circuit homework—and STEM problem sets more broadly—are hard not because students lack ability, but because they get stuck without feedback that matches how they learn. Traditional help is either too generic (same hints for everyone) or too permissive (AI that hands over answers).

## What Scafflow Is

**Scafflow** is a needs- and attention-aware AI scaffolding system for STEM homework. It does not replace coursework or act as a generic chatbot. Instead, it adapts **how students enter the same assigned problem**—how much structure, choice, feedback, and guidance they receive while they reason through it.

The goal is to make difficult STEM problems feel more approachable: students stay engaged, work through challenges, and remain motivated to learn—without turning AI support into an answer shortcut.

## What Scafflow Is Not

- Not a homework answer engine
- Not a one-size-fits-all online course platform
- Not a static worksheet with a chat box bolted on
- Not a replacement for instructors or independent reasoning

## The Promise (Elevator Pitch)

**Same assigned course problems. Multiple interaction paths. Support that adapts and fades**—keeping student reasoning and motivation under both student and instructor control.

## Initial Domain Focus

The first testbed is **electrical and computer engineering (ECE)**, centered on **circuit analysis**—topics such as Kirchhoff’s laws, mesh and nodal analysis, phasors, impedance, and Thévenin/Norton equivalents. The product concept generalizes to other STEM domains; the initial content and partnerships anchor in university ECE coursework (e.g., circuit theory at the University of Washington).

## Success Looks Like

- Students complete assigned problems while building transferable problem-solving skill—not just correct final values
- Struggling students get enough structure to stay in productive struggle instead of quitting or outsourcing reasoning
- Capable students are not slowed down by unnecessary hand-holding
- Instructors retain confidence that AI support aligns with course goals and academic integrity
- Learning gains persist beyond a single problem session
